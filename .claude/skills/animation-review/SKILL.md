# Animation Review Skill - Mission to the Moon

You review and repair how a flight plays: what moves, how fast, where the camera goes, and whether the words at the bottom of the screen describe what is on it.

**Scope:** motion and its timing. For the layout of the card and dock, work in `src/styles/main.css` directly. For how to write the tests a fix needs, use `test-planning`, `vitest-testing` and `e2e-testing`.

## Why this skill exists

The site's owner judges motion by watching it, and wants it calm: "I want this UI to not be distracting, but give the user a lot of information when they want it."

Motion problems here hide from the usual checks. In November 2026 a single moment ("Eagle undocks") took four rounds of fixes, and every round passed lint, types, 800 unit tests and the browser suite. The first three rounds were judged from still frames a second or more apart, and each fixed something real while missing what the owner was looking at. One 34-second screen recording then showed three problems in a minute: the camera lurched past the Moon when it changed view, the stop never showed the undocking it described, and the card named a moment the scene had not reached.

So the method is: **watch the motion the way a visitor sees it, write down what is on screen second by second, and compare that with the standards below, before touching code and again after.** Passing tests say the code does what it was written to do. They do not say it is pleasant to watch.

The first full audit with this skill (five reviewers over the whole of Apollo 11) found about sixty problems. The most frequent was a camera that moved by itself: it re-aimed when the card changed size, on Pause, and after a slow frame. None of them showed in a test.

**The lap limit is judged by the owner's eye, and "better" is not "fine".** The owner called a lap of the Moon in 4.4 seconds "way too fast" and one in 8.8 "better". The code then held a drawn craft to one lap in 8 seconds for three rounds, and with every orbit leaving and arriving at a lap in 6 to 10 seconds the owner said "the orbit animations are still too fast". The 20 and 30 seconds in standard 7 are working figures set after that, not numbers the owner gave; so is the 90 degrees a second in standard 11. Replace them when the owner says what reads as calm, and never quote them as theirs. The scene's fast-forward thresholds (`src/scene/fast-forward.ts`: when the ring comes on and goes off) have disagreed with standard 7 before, so a drawn craft could race. Judge by the standard, not by the constants; `pace.mjs` prints both.

When a report does not fit any row, add a row once the owner has confirmed what they wanted. This table is the memory of what "calm" means here.

## The procedure

### 1. Get a recording before forming a theory

- **If the owner sent a screen recording, cut it up first.** It is the only evidence of what they actually saw, including what they pressed.

  ```bash
  swift .claude/skills/animation-review/scripts/video-sheets.swift "<video>" <out-dir> overview 0 <length> 1 560 3 4
  ```

  That writes contact sheets, one frame a second, twelve to a sheet, each labelled with its time (for a phone's upright video use `400 6 2`, so a sheet stays under 2000 pixels a side and is not shrunk when read). Read them, find the seconds that matter, then cut those again at 0.25 s with a crop (the last argument is `x,y,width,height` as fractions of the frame) so the spacecraft and the card are legible. Watch the pointer too: a press on the tour's button mid-trip is a skip.

- **If there is no recording, make one and say so.** Ask the owner for a recording when the report is about feel ("odd", "not great") and your own recording does not show an obvious cause. Guessing costs a round trip each time.
- **Record the same sequence yourself**, so there is a before to compare with. Start a dev server of your own (`npx vite --port 5199 --strictPort`; set `BASE=http://localhost:<port>` for another port), then:

  ```bash
  R='node .claude/skills/animation-review/scripts/record.mjs'
  $R tour apollo-11 4 --out <dir>                       # the trip from stop 4, desktop
  $R tour apollo-11 4 --phone --out <dir>               # the same on a phone (390x844, dpr 2)
  $R tour apollo-11 6 --press press:back --out <dir>    # Back from stop 6
  $R explore apollo-11 undocking moon --after wait:5000 --after press:pause --out <dir>
  node .claude/skills/animation-review/scripts/pace.mjs apollo-11   # the pace table, no pictures
  ```

  One command records the sequence and checks it. On the tour it drives to the stop, says which stop it leaves from (and fails if that is not the one asked for), presses Next (or `--press`), runs any `--after` steps, and stops two seconds after everything is still. In Explore it opens the flight paused at an event or a mission time (`t=100:20:00`), in a view (`-` for the event's own), presses Play, and stops a little past the next event. `--before` and `--after` take one step each, as often as needed: Skip mid-trip, starting over, a drag, a pause, opening Controls, the next event, true scale, a deliberate stall. [references/cast-steps.md](references/cast-steps.md) has the command for each of these and every step.

  Each recording folder holds the build stamp, every frame, a timeline every 100 ms, the camera every frame, and what `check.py` and `sheets.py` make of them. [references/recording-files.md](references/recording-files.md) describes every file and column. Read them in this order:
  1. **`check.txt`'s signals.** `FAIL` means a standard is plainly broken (the card names a moment the clock has not reached); `LOOK` means the marks found something; `ok` means they found nothing, which is not a pass; `REDO` means too few frames came to judge motion by: record again before reading anything else.
  2. **The marks and their sheets** (`mark-NN-*.jpg`): each moment worth a look at 0.1 s, cropped around the craft or whole for a camera move.
  3. **`craft-NN.jpg` and `sheet-NN.jpg`**: the whole recording at 0.5 s, around the craft and whole.
  4. **`camera-table.tsv`** for the numbers behind a picture: turn and roll rates, the horizon's tilt, how much of the screen a body covers, where the craft is and whether it jumped, laps per second.

- **Check the build line.** It names the commit, how many files are changed, and whether the dev server serves the tree you are in. A warning there means the recording shows some other code. The line after gives the machine's load: on a busy machine the same trip recorded at 1.9 frames a second instead of 12, and the slow frames changed what the scene did.
- **`pace.mjs` answers pace questions without a picture.** It prints each event's story second; each stretch's mission seconds per story second at its ends, on average and at its fastest, and the fastest lapping of a drawn craft there at 1x; the fastest lapping within 3 story seconds of an event (and, of those, on a path that is no closed lap, where no ring can stand in); and each tour trip's and run-up's top pace and fastest lapping, flagged against standards 6 and 7. It reads the pace from the clock a moment at a time, and decides drawn or ring by the scene's own rules at each instant; the scene's cross-fade takes a moment to follow, so a short fast stretch (a run-up) can still show a drawn craft racing where the table has the ring. A craft on a closed lap stays drawn up to the scene's faster threshold, so a stretch easing into its middle passes a fraction of a second between the two thresholds; the flag says so. A recording's `7 laps` signal measures what was actually drawn.

Look at motion at several frames a second. Frames a second apart will not show a lurch that lasts half a second: that is exactly how the camera problem was missed three times. Headless Chrome draws with software WebGL, so it is slower than a real screen (about ten frames a second), but trips keep real time.

### 2. Write the sequence down

**On the tour,** make a table of the whole sequence, from the stop before to the stop after: the trip in, the arrival, the rest, the trip out. `tour <mission> all` records every trip in one run.

**In Explore,** make one row per event, in each view the flight offers: `explore <mission> all <view>` records each event in turn, paused at it, pressing Play, and running a couple of story seconds past the next one. The first second after Play from a pause behaves differently from continuous play (the fade and the camera start from rest), so each stretch is seen twice, once from its own Play and once at the end of the one before. Compare the two.

For each stretch note the seconds, what the clock covers, the view, whether fast forward shows, what is drawn, and what the card says; the `changes` section of `check.txt` gives most of it. Then go down the standards and mark each breach with its time range and the sheet that shows it.

Name each breach by class, because the class tells you which layer owns it:

| Class         | Typical cause                                                                     | Where it lives                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pace          | The clock covers too much mission time per second, or the tour squeezes a stretch | `src/sim/story-time.ts`, `src/app/player.ts` (`tripShape`), `src/app/tour.ts`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Camera path   | A change of view, or the return from the steady fast-forward view                 | `src/scene/stage.ts`: `applyFraming`, `startTween`, `movePose` and `calmestWay` (a move's path and which way round it goes), `updateCamera`, `followChase` (the ship view's chase), `updateFastView` and `setFastView` (into and out of the steady view); `src/scene/framing.ts`; `src/scene/config.ts` (`CAMERA_MOVE_MS`, `CAMERA_TURN_DEG_PER_S`) |
| Camera re-aim | The camera moves by itself in the Earth or Moon view                              | `src/scene/stage.ts`: `reframeAfterLayout` (the card or the window changes size), the re-aim after a pause or seek in `updateCamera` (when `shipHidden` says the craft is hidden), and how `tick` decides a frame was a jump (a slow frame can count as one)                                                                                        |
| Camera timing | The tour moves the camera at the wrong moment                                     | `src/app/tour.ts` (`VIEW_LEAD_SECONDS`, `HURRY_VIEW_MISSION_SECONDS`, `EARLY_VIEW_MISSION_SECONDS`, `CAMERA_SETTLE_MAX_SECONDS`, `watchScene`)                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

Read the constants where they are defined rather than trusting numbers quoted anywhere else, this file included; they are tuned often.

### 3. Fix at the layer that owns it, one class at a time

- Prefer the general rule over the special case. "Eagle undocks" was first treated as one flight's problem; the causes were rules that reach every flight.
- Before changing a shared rule, **count what it reaches** and say so: how many tour trips, which flights, Explore as well as the tour. `pace.mjs` over every mission answers this for pace in a minute or two; a throwaway script over every mission's story clock answers the rest (`src/test/test-utils.ts` and `src/app/tour.test.ts` show how to build a flight's clock outside the browser). One tour-pacing change reached 59 of 137 trips; one threshold change altered Explore at normal speed in a dozen flights.
- Keep the stated numbers honest. If a stop now rests a few minutes after its event, the clock in Controls reads later than the event's time: say so.

### 4. Record again, the same way

Run the same recording, on desktop and phone, and put before and after side by side: the signals, the timeline rows, and the sheets for each breach. For a sweep, `python3 .claude/skills/animation-review/scripts/check.py <sweep-folder>` prints one line a recording with each signal's state, before and after alike. Then look at the stretches on either side of the one you changed. A fix to the trip out often shows up as a new problem at the arrival.

Also check the cases a recording of the happy path skips: Skip pressed mid-trip, Back, starting the tour over, the end of the tour, the same moment reached in Explore by pressing Play, a pause, a drag, true scale, and reduced motion (`--reduced-motion`). Each is one command (see [references/cast-steps.md](references/cast-steps.md)).

### 5. Sweep for the same class elsewhere

If the cause was a rule, the same breach is waiting in other flights. Record the tours that exercise it (`tour <mission> all`; a Gemini flight for Earth orbit, another Apollo landing), and list the ones you did not watch. For standard 1 the card check alone is enough, and for the pace standards (5 to 8) and standard 19 `pace.mjs` gives the numbers, so sweeping every flight for those is cheap.

### 6. Tests

Pacing arithmetic and the tour's sequencing are pure and belong in unit tests: `src/app/tour.test.ts` runs the real tour and clock against a fake screen, and can assert what the camera was asked for and when, the clock's place at each moment, and how long a trip takes. Do not try to assert the feel of a camera move in a browser test; it is slow, flaky, and still would not tell you whether it looks right. Follow `test-planning` and `vitest-testing`.

### 7. Report what was watched

Say which sequences you watched, on which screens, with frames how far apart, from which build (the build line), and which you did not. Name the frames or sheets that show each claim. "All checks pass" is a statement about the tests; keep it separate from statements about how it looks, and keep "no marks" separate from "looks right". Give side effects on other flights their own list, and say plainly what still looks weak.

## How the pieces fit

Enough of the mechanism to read a timeline. Verify details in the code.

- **Story time** (`src/sim/story-time.ts`). The clock does not run at one rate. Each stretch between two key times (the events, and a few added keys) plays for 2 to 14 "story seconds" depending on its length, so a three-minute climb and a three-day coast both get watchable time. Explore at 1x plays one story second per real second. A short or quiet stretch plays at one even pace. A long one (faster than about 900 mission seconds per story second, played evenly) eases into and out of its events: its first and last 1.5 story seconds play at a gentle 500 (a low lunar orbit laps once in 14 seconds), a ramp of up to 2.6 story seconds at each end grows the pace with the square of the mission time (as a departing craft's turn round its body slows), and the middle makes up the time at about twice the even pace. So a day in lunar orbit is still about twelve laps in twelve seconds, nearly all of them in the middle, as the ring, and the craft is drawn at a watchable pace either side of each event. Every key keeps its story second, so trips and the timeline are unchanged. The `EASE_*` constants hold the numbers; `pace.mjs --even` shows a flight's clock as it was before.
- **Fast forward** (`src/scene/stage.ts`, `updateFastForward`; the rules in `src/scene/fast-forward.ts`). A craft lapping its body faster than a threshold on screen is replaced by a ring in its own colour, with a short cross-fade, but only while its path around now is a closed lap: a craft leaving orbit, arriving, or climbing from the surface stays drawn. Every craft in fast forward gets its own ring. For the followed craft the ship view also holds a steady wide view (`updateFastView`), reached and left by one camera move each way (`setFastView`): in as the craft goes into fast forward, a moment before the interface says "Fast forward", and out as it stops saying so. `fastForward` in the scene report is true while the followed craft is in fast forward, in any view, and each ship in the report carries its own `fastForward`.
- **The guided tour** (`src/app/tour.ts`). Next flies a trip to the next stop; Back and starting play a short run-up at Explore's pace, no longer than the last 30 minutes before the stop, or cut straight to the stop when that is under a story second (the `RUN_UP_*` constants). Since the clock eases into its events, that happens only at a stop at the very start of the clock. A trip takes as long as its story lasts, between a minimum and a maximum. A longer story is squeezed: Explore's pace for the first and last few seconds, a hurry between (`tripShape` in `src/app/player.ts`). The card turns to the next stop as the trip begins. The camera moves to the stop's view shortly before arriving, with rules for fast forward; on a hurried trip out of the ship view to a stop in the Earth or Moon view a few hours off, it moves as the hurry begins instead, so the trip has one camera move and the run-in to the stop is seen from a still camera (`HURRY_VIEW_MISSION_SECONDS`). On arriving the tour waits for the camera to settle before it offers Next (`CAMERA_SETTLE_MAX_SECONDS`); a Skip stops at once. Some stops rest a little after their event so the event's action is seen (the `PARTING_*` constants), holding the clock on the event first while the camera settles.
- **Views** (`src/scene/stage.ts`). Earth, Moon, ship ("spacecraft", a chase view of the followed craft) and journey. Craft are drawn at a fixed size on screen, not to scale. A move between views takes 1.5 to 3 seconds, as long as its turn of the picture needs (`CAMERA_MOVE_MS`, `CAMERA_TURN_DEG_PER_S` in `src/scene/config.ts`), and reports itself in `moving`. The framing fits the clear area the interface leaves; when that changes (the card grows), `reframeAfterLayout` moves the camera, in distance only in the Earth and Moon views.
- **Pairs.** Two craft docked, parting or keeping station are drawn together with a gap that opens and closes in story time; the flight's phases (`kind: 'station'`, `'docked'`) say when.

Numbers for sanity checks: a low lunar orbit takes about two hours (7,100 seconds); a low Earth orbit about ninety minutes. Laps per second on screen is the mission seconds the clock covers per real second, divided by the period.

## Things that have gone wrong before

- **Judging from the wrong build.** The owner tested while a fix was half-saved. The dev server serves the working tree as it is saved, so while a fix is under way the page can show behaviour that belongs to neither the old code nor the new. Say when a build is ready, and not before; check each recording's build line.
- **Fixing the reported moment only.** The trip out was slowed while the lurch was on the trip in and the mismatch was on the card.
- **Reading "too fast" as the spacecraft.** Much of it was the camera. Look at the camera's path first.
- **Then reading it as the camera only.** With the camera fixed, the owner still said the orbits were too fast: every drawn lap at 6 to 10 seconds was the spacecraft's own pace. Check both, and re-read a recording's lap figures against standard 7 rather than against whatever the code allows.
- **Taking "better" for "fine".** A lap in 8.8 seconds was called better than 4.4, and that became the limit for three rounds. A comparison is not an acceptance; ask, or aim well past it.
- **Trusting a transcript of numbers.** A timeline said the view changed at 11.5 s; only frames showed the Moon filling the screen on the way.
- **Reporting a pass as a result.** Every broken round had passed every check.
- **Taking a slow frame for a matter of smoothness only.** A frame that comes late can change what the scene does, not just how smooth it looks: the stage may take it for a jump of the clock and re-aim the camera or snap a fade. Record a suspicious trip twice, and try a deliberate stall (`--after stall:400`) to see whether the behaviour follows the stall.
- **Running recorders side by side.** Two at once halve each other's frame rate, and the stalls that causes look like the app's. Record one thing at a time. On a shared machine others' recordings slow yours: record.mjs warns when the load is above the number of cores, and `check.py` says `REDO` when frames came too slowly to judge. Wait until `uptime` shows the load has dropped, then record again.
- **Looking for true scale on the tour.** Controls (and the True scale switch) are hidden on the tour. Turn it on with `--before 'eval:window.stage.setScaleMode("true")'`, the call the switch makes, and check the timeline's `scale` column.
- **Using `window.stage` outside development.** It exists only in a development build, and until the Stage is made it is the canvas element of the same id. A recording against a production build has no clock, camera or fade columns.
